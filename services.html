Support for Children with ASD & ADHD

The Five Main Therapy Disciplines

CRCT provides focused developmental support for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and ADHD through five primary therapy disciplines: Occupational Therapy, Behavioural Therapy, Speech Therapy, Sensory Integration Therapy, and Special Education.

Professional Disclaimer: Information on this website is for general educational purposes only and does not replace professional assessment, diagnosis or individualised advice.
Core Clinical Offering

Focused Child Development Support

Each child has a unique developmental profile. We do not assume that every child requires all five therapies. Instead, individual assessment determines which disciplines are relevant to support your child's functional goals.

Therapy Area 1

Occupational Therapy

Occupational Therapy (OT) helps children build the physical, motor, and functional abilities needed to navigate their everyday world with confidence and greater independence.

How Occupational Therapy May Support Your Child

  • Fine motor skills: Developing finger strength and hand-eye coordination for grasping pencils, using safety scissors, turning pages, and handling small toys.
  • Gross motor coordination: Improving balance, postural stability, bilateral movement, jumping, and coordinating whole-body physical activities.
  • Daily living activities & self-care: Supporting independence in self-feeding with spoons, unbuttoning/buttoning, pulling zippers, washing hands, and dressing.
  • Play and functional activities: Developing motor planning skills required to engage comfortably in constructive, imaginative, and peer-based play.
  • Participation in everyday environments: Assisting children to comfortably manage school classroom demands, playground activities, and home tasks.
  • Individual sensory and functional needs: Integrating movement-based strategies to help children maintain focus, comfort, and physical regulation.

What Parents May Notice

Parents often observe subtle functional challenges during daily routines at home or school:

  • Difficulty with dressing, such as fastening buttons, managing zippers, pulling on socks, or tying shoelaces.
  • Struggling to handle small objects, frequently dropping crayons, beads, or small toy parts.
  • Difficulty using utensils (spoons or forks) during meal times, resulting in frequent spills or awkward grips.
  • Tiring quickly during coloring, drawing, or handwriting activities due to hand fatigue or weak grasp.
  • Hesitation or clumsiness when climbing stairs, riding a tricycle, or participating in playground games.

Assessment & Goal Setting: An occupational therapist conducts a gentle observational assessment of the child's movement, hand function, and daily living skills to set realistic, age-appropriate goals tailored to the child's lifestyle.

Therapy Area 2

Behavioural Therapy

Behavioural therapy at CRCT is centered on positive, individualized support that respects the child's dignity, personal comfort, and communication style while building practical everyday life skills.

How Behavioural Therapy May Support Your Child

  • Communication-related behaviours: Helping children learn clear, effective ways to express their needs, preferences, and feelings rather than experiencing overwhelming frustration.
  • Learning useful daily skills: Supporting self-help routines, mealtime habits, hygiene steps, and personal organisation through manageable, positive steps.
  • Social participation: Fostering turn-taking, parallel play, sharing space with peers, and positive interactions in social settings.
  • Following routines & transitions: Easing distress during schedule changes, moving between activities, or adjusting to new environments using visual schedules.
  • Understanding triggers & environment: Identifying sensory overload, fatigue, or communication barriers that trigger distress or meltdowns.
  • Developing alternative coping skills: Guiding children to functional alternative responses and calming strategies when feeling overwhelmed.

Understanding Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA)

Clinical Confirmation Required

Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) is a structured, evidence-informed framework that uses positive reinforcement to teach meaningful communication and functional daily living skills.

Our Clinical Approach to ABA: At CRCT, an ABA programme approach may be considered for some children in the 2–5-year age group, particularly following early developmental screening and comprehensive clinical assessment.

Important Note: We do not state that every child aged 2–5 automatically requires ABA, nor do we claim that ABA is appropriate for every child. This programme approach requires confirmation from CRCT's clinical team.

All behavioural goals must respect the child's dignity, bodily autonomy, communication preferences, and emotional wellbeing. We strictly oppose forced masking or repetitive compliance drills.

Therapy Area 3

Speech Therapy

Speech and Language Therapy supports children with Autism and ADHD to develop meaningful ways to express themselves, comprehend language, and connect with people around them.

How Speech Therapy May Support Your Child

  • Expressive communication: Supporting vocabulary development, sentence building, vocal expression, and articulating thoughts clearly.
  • Understanding language: Helping children process spoken instructions, understand questions, and interpret classroom or household requests.
  • Speech and language development: Addressing articulation delays, rhythm of speech, and oral motor coordination.
  • Communicating needs and feelings: Providing children with functional methods to state preferences, ask for help, or indicate pain without distress.
  • Social communication (pragmatics): Building conversational turn-taking, joint attention, interpreting intent, and engaging in back-and-forth exchanges.
  • Gestures & non-verbal communication: Encouraging pointing, head-nodding, facial expressions, and natural body language.
  • Alternative and Augmentative Communication (AAC): Introducing visual timetables, picture cards (PECS), and communication boards where appropriate and actually offered by CRCT.

Everyday Situations Parents May Recognise

Communication differences often reveal themselves in everyday household and social moments:

  • A child struggling to express essential needs—such as wanting water, food, or rest—leading to frustration, tears, or pulling an adult's hand.
  • Difficulty understanding verbal instructions or needing steps repeated multiple times before taking action.
  • Repeating dialogue, rhymes, or movie phrases (echolalia) without using spoken words for interactive conversation.
  • Reluctance or difficulty initiating greetings, maintaining conversational turns, or responding when their name is called.
  • Relying exclusively on non-verbal gestures and needing structured tools to expand communication options.

Speech therapy sessions are designed around playful, interactive activities that make communication enjoyable, low-pressure, and functionally rewarding.

Therapy Area 4 In-Depth Educational Guide

Sensory Integration Therapy

Sensory processing differences play a central role in how children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and ADHD experience the world. Sensory Integration Therapy helps children process and organize environmental inputs so they can feel regulated, secure, and ready to participate in daily life.

Sensory Systems Explained

Understanding Your Child's Sensory World

Our bodies process sensory information through multiple interconnected neurological pathways. When sensory messages are processed differently, everyday environments can feel overwhelming or under-stimulating.

1. The Vestibular System (Balance & Movement)

What it does: Located in the inner ear, it senses balance, head position, motion, and gravity. It tells the brain whether the body is moving, how fast, and in which direction.

Possible parent observations:

  • A child who avoids playground swings, slides, carousels, or sudden movement.
  • Discomfort, anxiety, or motion sickness during position changes (e.g. tilting backwards).
  • Alternatively, frequently seeking intense movement—spinning, running in circles, or rocking.

Everyday impact: Affects physical play confidence, stair navigation, balance during sports, and comfortable participation in group games.

2. The Proprioceptive System (Body Awareness & Force)

What it does: Located in muscles, joints, and tendons, it provides subconscious awareness of body position and helps judge the amount of force needed for physical tasks.

Possible parent observations:

  • Pushing doors or objects with excessive force, or pressing pencils so hard that lead breaks.
  • Jumping frequently off furniture, crashing into cushions, or craving tight hugs/deep pressure.
  • Difficulty judging personal space, bumping into people, or misjudging grip strength on delicate items.

Everyday impact: Influences physical coordination, sitting still in chairs, handling toys gently, and overall body confidence.

3. The Tactile System (Touch & Textures)

What it does: Receives sensations through the skin including light touch, deep pressure, texture, pain, and temperature.

Possible parent observations:

  • Strong distress from clothing labels, tight waistbands, sock seams, or specific fabrics.
  • Intense resistance to grooming routines such as hair washing, haircutting, or nail trimming.
  • Avoidance of messy play (finger paint, clay, sand, glue) or hesitation to touch wet textures.

Everyday impact: Can create stress during morning dressing, hygiene routines, and tactile school activities.

4. The Auditory System (Sound Processing)

What it does: Processes sound frequency, pitch, volume, and background noise, filtering essential speech from ambient sounds.

Possible parent observations:

  • Covering ears in response to vacuum cleaners, mixer-grinders, traffic horns, or school bells.
  • High discomfort or distress in busy, noisy environments like shopping malls, weddings, or dining halls.
  • Difficulty following spoken directions when there is background television or conversation.

Everyday impact: Influences listening focus in busy classrooms and participation in family celebrations.

5. The Visual System (Visual Processing)

What it does: Interprets light, movement, spatial relationships, contrast, and visual patterns.

Possible parent observations:

  • Discomfort, shielding eyes, or squinting under harsh fluorescent lighting or bright outdoor sunlight.
  • Feeling overwhelmed in visually cluttered rooms, superstores, or visually chaotic environments.
  • Fascination with repetitive visual motions such as ceiling fans, spinning wheels, or tilting objects against light.

Everyday impact: Affects reading endurance, visual focus during schoolwork, and settling down before sleep.

How Sensory Differences May Affect Everyday Life

When sensory inputs are processed with heightened intensity (hypersensitivity) or reduced intensity (hyposensitivity), some children may experience discomfort, distress, avoidance, stress, or anxiety in certain environments. For example, a classroom with buzzing lights and echoing chatter may cause a child to withdraw, cover their ears, or experience emotional overload.

Important Clinical Boundaries:
  • Sensory differences do not automatically cause phobias.
  • A child cannot and must not be diagnosed from individual sensory behaviours.
  • Examples described above are illustrative possibilities, not universal symptoms for every child.
Individual Sensory Profiles:
  • Each child's sensory profile is entirely unique; one child may be sound-sensitive while seeking deep pressure.
  • Sensory integration therapy supports coping techniques and environmental accommodations.
  • We do not claim that sensory integration therapy guarantees particular medical outcomes or cures.
Therapy Area 5

Special Education

Special Education at CRCT focuses on adapted, individualized learning that meets children at their current developmental level, building foundational cognitive and pre-academic readiness skills.

How Special Education May Support Your Child

  • Individualised learning: Structuring activities according to the child's learning style, pacing, and current developmental abilities.
  • Attention and participation: Fostering sustained table-top engagement, listening focus, and active involvement in learning tasks.
  • Pre-academic skills: Preparing children for structured learning through pencil holding, orientation, visual tracking, and following step-by-step directions.
  • Cognitive & foundational learning: Building understanding of concepts, cause-and-effect relationships, and basic problem solving.
  • Matching, sorting & recognition: Hands-on activities involving color sorting, shape matching, size comparison, object identification, and pattern recognition.
  • Communication-related learning: Pairing visual symbols, pictures, and words to expand functional understanding.
  • Classroom readiness: Developing sitting tolerance, transitions between activities, and adapting to structured school routines.
  • Functional educational goals: Setting practical, age-appropriate developmental milestones that make learning enjoyable and rewarding.

Adapting to Your Child's Learning Style

Every child absorbs information in distinct ways. Some children are highly visual learners who thrive on illustrated cards, while others learn through tactile manipulatives, repetitive songs, or structured routines. Special educators tailor learning activities to fit the child's natural strengths.

Scope & Clarification Notice:

CRCT offers developmental learning support, pre-academic readiness, and cognitive skill building. We do not provide mainstream school admission guarantees, formal IEP government certifications, or government-approved educational accreditations.

Developmental Guidance

Early Support Can Make a Difference.

Early identification of developmental concerns allows families to seek appropriate professional guidance during foundational years.

Why Early Support Matters: During early childhood, the brain is exceptionally receptive to learning new ways of communicating, moving, and regulating emotions. When developmental concerns in speech, social communication, motor skills, or sensory processing are identified early, appropriate professional guidance can help children develop healthy coping mechanisms and independence.

Individualized Support Across Domains: Individualized support may assist children across multiple developmental areas, including communication, learning, behaviour, and everyday daily living skills. Because each child is unique, therapy plans should be tailored based on that specific child's needs. Not every child requires all five therapies.

Valuable Parental Partnership: When parents are actively involved, strategies practiced during therapy can be carried forward naturally into the family routine, reinforcing the child's sense of security and progress.

Balanced, Fear-Free Support

We believe in calm, compassionate guidance. We avoid fear-based statements such as claims that a child's future is damaged if action is delayed. Nor do we promise a cure or guaranteed normal development. Progress happens step by step through patient, evidence-informed support.

ABA Programme Note: For some children in the 2–5-year age group, an Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) programme may be considered following early intervention screening, subject to confirmation from CRCT's clinical team.

Therapist Commitment

Dedicated Therapists. Individual Attention. Meaningful Progress.

Every child at CRCT is supported by dedicated therapists who focus on the whole child, building a safe and trusting therapeutic relationship.

Understanding Individual Needs

Observing the child's sensory triggers, communication styles, emotional responses, and personal strengths before planning.

Setting Suitable Goals

Collaborating with parents to set realistic, age-appropriate developmental milestones that support daily life.

Focused Therapy Sessions

Conducting dedicated sessions utilizing evidence-informed strategies and playful activities in quiet, sensory-friendly spaces.

Reviewing & Communicating

Continuously observing responses, reviewing progress, communicating relevant observations with parents, and adjusting support.

* This represents CRCT's confirmed clinical approach to therapy delivery. All consultations and therapy sessions are conducted on prior appointment only to preserve a calm, dedicated clinical environment.

Clinical Flow

How Our Clinical Sessions Work

Every developmental pathway is distinct. Here is what families can expect when consulting CRCT:

01

Initial Family Consultation

A relaxed, unhurried discussion with parents to listen to developmental history, daily joys, triggers, and family observations.

02

Gentle Child Observation

Play-based interaction in a sensory-friendly space assessing sensory preferences, communication modes, motor skills, and strengths.

03

Customized Therapy Roadmap

A collaborative action plan selecting the relevant therapies among Occupational, Behavioural, Speech, Sensory Integration, or Special Education.
