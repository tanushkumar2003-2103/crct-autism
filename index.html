Autism & ADHD Awareness

Understanding Autism Spectrum Disorder & ADHD

Every child develops along their own distinct timeline. We help families understand neurodevelopmental differences with warmth, clarity, and personalized guidance.

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) involves natural variations in communication, social interaction, and sensory processing. Autistic children have distinct strengths alongside areas where structured support can help.

ADHD is a developmental difference related to attention regulation, executive function, and activity levels. Some children have both Autism and ADHD (AuDHD).

Because each child's profile is unique, individual assessment matters. Therapy is customized to specific developmental needs rather than a generalized formula.

Key Insights for Parents

  • Natural variations: Autism and ADHD are lifelong neurological differences, not illnesses to cure.
  • Tailored support: Therapy goals always respect the child's dignity, wellbeing, and individual pace.
  • Parent partnership: Therapists and parents work hand-in-hand to build everyday confidence.

Individual Variation

Areas of Developmental Difference

Autism and ADHD present uniquely in every child. Rather than seeing differences as deficits, we observe how children experience the world, interact with others, and develop everyday life skills.

Communication & Speech

Differences in expressive speech, language comprehension, conversational flow, non-verbal expression, or reading social cues. Clear, patient communication supports growth.

Sensory Processing

Responses to sounds, textures, lights, balance, and body awareness (proprioception). Children may experience sensory overload or actively seek movement to feel grounded.

Attention & Regulation

Variations in sustained attention, impulse control, shifting focus between tasks, and energy levels. Visual schedules and structured routines provide valuable clarity.

Motor Skills & Daily Living

Coordination, balance, fine motor tasks such as holding utensils or pencils, and self-care skills like buttoning and dressing that support personal independence.

Note: These observations are general educational examples and should not be used as a diagnostic checklist. A comprehensive clinical evaluation is essential to understand your child's individual profile.

Developmental Foundations

Early Support Can Make a Difference.

Timely guidance during formative years helps children build essential communication, motor, and behavioural skills with confidence.

Every child's path is unique. Professional guidance helps clarify needs early without fear, pressure, or alarmist predictions:

  • Individualised plans: Support tailored to your child's stage and practical daily needs.
  • Functional skills: Helping children communicate, regulate sensations, and build independence.
  • Active parent partnership: Practical strategies to carry therapy naturally into home routines.
  • Specialised programmes: Age-appropriate approaches (including ABA for ages 2–5) considered upon clinical confirmation.

Five Main Therapies

Support for Children with ASD & ADHD

CRCT provides focused developmental support for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and ADHD through five primary therapy disciplines, each tailored to the child's individual assessment.

Therapy Area 1

1. Occupational Therapy

Supports fine motor coordination, gross motor skills, everyday self-care (dressing, eating, grooming), play activities, and building independence in daily home and school environments.

Therapy Area 2

2. Behavioural Therapy

Positive, individualized support to develop useful daily skills, social participation, and routine following. Includes Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) for children aged 2–5 where clinically confirmed.

Therapy Area 3

3. Speech Therapy

Supports expressive communication, language comprehension, social communication, gestures, and augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) where appropriate.

Therapy Area 4

4. Sensory Integration Therapy

Addresses vestibular (balance), proprioceptive (body awareness), tactile, auditory, and visual processing differences, helping children feel calm, regulated, and engaged in everyday activities.

5. Special Education

Individualized learning adaptations, pre-academic skills, cognitive matching and sorting, attention building, and classroom readiness tailored to the child's learning style.

Our Clinical Approach

Dedicated Therapists. Individual Attention. Meaningful Progress.

At CRCT, our clinical approach is grounded in dedicated attention to each child's unique developmental path. We believe genuine progress happens when therapists understand the whole child and work hand-in-hand with parents.

Understanding Individual Needs

Taking the time to observe each child's communication style, sensory triggers, developmental stage, and innate strengths.

Setting Suitable Goals

Establishing realistic, functional developmental milestones that respect the child's dignity, pace, and daily living priorities.

Focused Therapy Sessions

Using evidence-informed strategies, playful activities, and calming spaces to keep children actively engaged and secure.

Reviewing Progress with Parents

Observing the child's responses, sharing relevant observations with parents, and adjusting support as the child develops.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about autism, seeking guidance, and scheduling support with CRCT.

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) primarily shapes how a child experiences communication, processes sensory input, and interacts socially, often accompanied by deep focus on specific interests and a preference for predictable routines. ADHD (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder) affects executive functioning, attention regulation, impulse control, and activity levels. While both are neurodevelopmental differences, they influence day-to-day activities and learning styles in distinct ways.

Yes. Clinical research confirms that Autism and ADHD frequently co-occur (often referred to informally as AuDHD). A child with both may experience contrasting needs—such as craving routine and order (Autism) while also seeking novelty, high energy, and stimulation (ADHD). An individualized assessment is essential to understand how these traits interact in each child.

CRCT focuses on five primary developmental therapy disciplines for children with ASD and ADHD: 1. Occupational Therapy (fine/gross motor, self-care, daily activities), 2. Behavioural Therapy (positive skill building, routines, ABA principles), 3. Speech Therapy (expressive and receptive communication, social communication, AAC), 4. Sensory Integration Therapy (vestibular, proprioceptive, tactile, auditory, and visual processing), and 5. Special Education (adapted learning, pre-academic skills, and classroom readiness).

No. We do not apply a blanket formula. Each child has distinct developmental strengths, sensory profiles, and challenges. A thorough individual assessment by our clinical team determines which specific therapies are relevant. Some children may benefit primarily from Occupational Therapy and Speech Therapy, while others may require Behavioural or Sensory Integration support.

ABA is a programme approach that may be considered for some children in the 2–5-year age group following early developmental screening and assessment. However, it is not automatically required for every child in that age group and requires formal confirmation from CRCT's clinical team. All behavioural interventions must respect the child's dignity, communication, and emotional wellbeing.
